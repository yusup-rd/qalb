# Qalb legal and data audit — release notes

**Reviewed: 19 October 2026.** These notes document repository behavior, not a legal opinion. The policy and terms files are drafts and are not hosted or linked as public URLs.

## Verified repository behavior

- No account, analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting integration was found in the inspected mobile/backend source and dependency manifests.
- Foreground location permission is requested by the mobile location initializer on app startup. Coordinates are held in an in-memory Zustand store, used for on-device prayer/Qibla calculations, and sent to the backend for geocoding and nearby mosques. Routing sends origin/destination coordinates. There is no background location request. Denying permission makes dependent features unavailable.
- Theme, language, prayer settings, Quran display flags, tasbih/spiritual-pause state, last-opened Surah, compass haptics, and API cache are locally persisted through AsyncStorage. Quran data is loaded from the bundled SQLite asset. Prayer alerts are scheduled locally through `expo-notifications`; no remote push provider or token flow was found.
- The backend can cache results in Redis, if `REDIS_URL` is configured. Code-defined TTLs are 15 minutes for mosque results, one hour for routes, and 24 hours for reverse-geocoding, Islamic calendar, and metal-price results. Request logs redact URLs, query strings, parameters, authorization, cookies, and referrers; deployment-host IP logging and retention are unknown. Logs may contain exception metadata.
- Runtime third-party integrations found: Nominatim/OpenStreetMap, public OSRM, TakbeerTime, AlAdhan, metals.dev; `react-native-maps` (Apple Maps by default on iOS and configured Google Maps on Android); QuranicAudio audio URLs; and Expo Updates. Their production configuration, contractual terms, and retention practices must be confirmed against deployed environments.
- The bundled Quran database contains resource IDs 85 (English translation), 45 (Russian translation), 57 (English transliteration), and reciter ID 6. The seed script obtains snapshots from Quran.Foundation’s regular Content API. The recitation URL path identifies the reciter as Husary and streams from `mirrors.quranicaudio.com`. The database itself does not preserve translation edition/author metadata or a verifiable license record.

## Release blockers and actions

1. **Quran Foundation content rights and distribution method.** Quran.Foundation’s Developer Terms, updated 11 October 2026, say their content may be shown within an app subject to terms, but a prepackaged database/build-time bundle is not authorized by the Content Sync exception. The repository’s seed script uses regular API snapshots and commits a bundled SQLite copy. Stop public distribution of this asset until Quran.Foundation confirms a written license or the app migrates to an approved content-sync method for every resource and implements required seven-day sync/update behavior. Confirm whether translation resources 85/45 and transliteration 57 have separate rights and obtain their author/edition names. Confirm QuranicAudio’s audio licensing separately.
2. **Public legal pages and store disclosures.** The user chose draft-only; there is no public domain or deployment target. Before release, publish stable HTTPS English and Russian policies, link them from Settings/About and store listings, and complete Apple App Privacy and Google Play Data safety forms for the production app and all SDK/provider handling.
3. **Identity and jurisdiction.** The developer is based in Turkmenistan; the publisher’s legal identity/address and target distribution markets are not established in the repository. Turkmenistan’s Law “On Information on Private Life and its Protection” (20 March 2017) defines personal information broadly, sets consent and data-minimization principles, and gives people rights to access, correct, block, and in certain cases destroy their information. The law also restricts processing of sensitive categories including relationship to religion, subject to stated exceptions. Qalb does not keep account or Quran-search histories, but the location and network data it processes can relate to an identifiable person. A Turkmenistan-qualified lawyer should determine how the app’s religious subject matter, app use, permission flows, consent evidence, operator identity/address, cross-border processing, retention, and request-response workflow are governed by the law. Confirm requirements in each distribution market too. The 2024 Malaysian amendments are not assumed to apply merely because the original prompt mentioned Malaysia.
4. **Production infrastructure.** Identify the actual `EXPO_PUBLIC_API_URL`, hosting company, Redis provider, proxy/CDN, log retention, and data-region settings. Confirm production provider endpoints, privacy terms, and retention. The source defaults include a local API URL and public routing/geocoding services; defaults do not prove production configuration.
5. **Permission notice.** The iOS location permission string currently says location is used to calculate prayer times, but the app also uses it for Qibla, automatically resolving the displayed locality, nearby mosques, and routes; some of those coordinates are sent to the Qalb API and onward to providers. Update and localize the permission notice and provide a clear in-app explanation before release. The permission is currently requested at launch.
6. **Third-party/open-source notices.** The repository root `LICENSE` is the Expo MIT license, with Expo copyright. It does not establish Qalb’s own source-code license and does not grant Quran or audio rights. Confirm the intended Qalb code license and provide notices for dependencies and data before publication.
7. **Donations.** No purchase SDK, store products, or transaction code exists. Expo SDK 57 documents `expo-iap` and `react-native-purchases` as options requiring native code and a development build. Apple permits tipping through in-app purchase currencies; Google’s standard Play Billing policy applies to most in-app digital payments, with listed exceptions including tax-exempt donations and peer-to-peer payments. Do not expose payment links or claim a donation can be made until a store-compliant product and account configuration are implemented and tested.
